# The recurring character

## Definition

A small, solid black character is the fixed visual anchor of this companion-illustration style.

It appears by default on every image. It's not a mascot, not a sticker, not a cute decoration, but a
small absurd operator that seriously takes part in the workings of the represented system.

## Appearance

- Small, solid, uniform black creature
- White dot eyes
- Thin legs, sometimes thin arms
- The body can take the shape of a cylinder, a black bean, a black box, a funnel, a shadow, a hole,
  a black block inside a machine
- Slightly irregular outline, with a hand-drawn texture
- Blank, impassive, calm, serious expression

## Personality

- Very serious, but what it does is a bit absurd
- Like a discreet little system operator
- Deadpan humor, never cute or "adorable"
- A bit clumsy, but not stupid
- As if it genuinely occupies a workstation in a whiteboard sketch

## Common responsibilities

Have the character carry the image's central action:

- Carrying equipment
- Pulling a line to gather information sources
- Stuck at a breaking point
- Pulling a "decision" lever inside a machine
- Turning into a sorting funnel
- Cutting up a "material fish"
- Stamping a transmission formula
- Holding the thread of a transmission path
- Holding up a warning sign in front of a trap
- Reaching out from a hole without managing to grab the content
- Alongside: carrying bricks, building a bridge, opening a door, sorting, saving

## Forbidden

- Never turn it into an overly cute mascot
- Never turn it into a children's cartoon character
- Never give it a complex costume, a sticker expression, shiny eyes
- Never leave it just standing in a corner watching
- Never let it steal the spotlight from the structure it's meant to illustrate
- Never draw it too commercial, too round, too polished

## Judgment criterion

If, after removing the character, the image's central metaphor still stands perfectly on its own,
then it was merely decorative. The prompt then needs rework so it becomes the active subject of the
action again.
